\chapter{Supporting Information for Chapter~\ref*{ch:temporal}}\label{app:ch5_si}

This appendix documents how the behavioural-model parameter cards of
\cref{sec:ch5_model} are sourced from the measured archive, and lists the
simulation and read-out settings used across the three evidence tiers. Together
with the per-cell tables of \cref{app:ch4_celltables}, it makes the in-silico
results reproducible without consulting the analysis scripts
(\path{scripts/ch5_figures.py}, \path{scripts/ch5_physio_context.py},
\path{scripts/ch5_onset.py}, \path{scripts/ch5_deployment.py},
\path{scripts/ch5_noneeg.py}).

\section{Parameter-Card Sourcing}\label{app:ch5_cards}

Each composition cell is reduced to a parameter card
$(\tau_c,\beta_c,\alpha_c,\text{peak}_c)$ that drives one reservoir node type. The
values are taken directly from the comparative fits, with no free re-tuning:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the fading-memory law $\lambda_c(\Delta t)=\exp[-(\Delta t/\tau_c)^{\beta_c}]$ uses the per-cell $(\tau_c,\beta_c)$ of \cref{tab:ch4_decay_by_cell}; for the few cells without an identified stretched fit, a single exponential reproducing the model-free half-life is used instead ($\tau_c=t_{1/2}/\ln 2$, $\beta_c=1$);
  \item the write nonlinearity uses the per-cell growth exponent $\alpha_c$ and peak enhancement of \cref{tab:ch4_pulses_by_cell};
  \item the device-to-device spread injected into each bank is the within-cell scatter of those same tables.
\end{itemize}
The heterogeneous banks of \cref{sec:ch5_benchmarks,sec:ch5_physio} span the
replicated \PEO{}/\LiTf{} composition cells of \cref{tab:ch4_decay_by_cell}
($\tau$ from $\approx 3$ to $\approx 26$\,s); the homogeneous control banks are
jittered copies of the lead cell \PEO{}~0.3/0.09 alone.

\section{Simulation and Read-out Settings}\label{app:ch5_settings}

\Cref{tab:ch5_settings} collects the settings used throughout
\cref{ch:temporal}. All read-outs are a single linear ridge regression; no
nonlinear decoder is used, so any demonstrated computation is attributable to the
device nodes rather than the read-out. The read-out ridge penalty
($\lambda=10^{-3}$), the causal label-smoothing horizon (\SI{15}{\second}), the bank
size $N$, and the reservoir step $\Delta t$ are fixed \emph{a priori} and held
identical across all banks and all held-out subjects; none is selected on the
held-out subject, so the leave-one-subject-out protocol carries no hyperparameter
leakage.

\section{Measured Device-to-Device Scatter}\label{app:ch5_scatter}

The robustness analysis of \cref{sec:ch5_deploy} (\cref{fig:ch5_robustness})
injects device-to-device variation as a multiplicative log-normal \emph{jitter} on
each node's timescale, \(\tau_i=\tau\,e^{\varepsilon}\) with
\(\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\); the jitter magnitude \(\sigma\) is, by
construction, the standard deviation of \(\ln\tau\) across nominally identical
devices. That quantity is measured directly here, so the dotted ``measured
scatter'' line in \cref{fig:ch5_robustness} is data-derived rather than assumed.

The estimate uses the fading-memory time constants of every silver
\PEO{}/\LiTf{} device (\(n=31\)). A naive spread taken across the whole
\textsf{SY,~PEO,~LiTr} group conflates the \emph{deliberate} \PEO{}\,\(\times\)\,salt
tuning sweep of \cref{ch:comparative} with random scatter and over-states it
(\(\sigma\approx\num{1.59}\)); the meaningful quantity is the spread \emph{within}
each fixed \PEO{}/salt cell, \(\sigma(\ln\tau)\approx\num{0.85}\)
(\cref{tab:ch5_scatter}). This residual is genuine device-to-device variation: it
is essentially unchanged after regressing out spin-coating speed and annealing
(\(\approx\num{0.91}\)), and within-cell residuals show no monotonic dependence on
film thickness (Spearman \(\rho\approx\num{0.14}\), \(n=31\), complete
profilometry) or on device age (\(\rho\approx\num{0.05}\)). The measured value is
roughly seven times the figure previously annotated on the robustness plot, and
the heterogeneous bank retains its memory-capacity advantage at it
(\cref{fig:ch5_robustness}), so the realised material diversity is, if anything,
larger than the simulations assume. The audit is reproduced by
\texttt{scripts/ch5\_scatter\_audit.py}.

\section{Continuous-Monitoring Stress Test}\label{app:ch5_robust}

The deployment-robustness result of \cref{sec:ch5_robust} (\path{scripts/ch5_onset.py})
is a continuous binary stress detector: at every $\Delta t = \SI{1}{\second}$ step the
read-out predicts stress (\WESAD{} label~2) versus not-stress (baseline and amusement
pooled), leave-one-subject-out, with the same single linear ridge read-out and
$15$-second causal label smoothing used in the streaming task. Four matched banks of
$N=24$ nodes are compared on identical input masks: the instantaneous input (no
memory), a memoryless bank (leak set to zero, dimensionality only), the homogeneous
lead-only bank, and the heterogeneous composition bank.

Sensor corruption is modelled by adding, to the per-subject scaled streams
(channels in $[0,1]$), independent Gaussian noise of standard deviation $\sigma$ at
every sample, plus sparse motion-artefact bursts (short windows of amplified noise at
a $1\%$ per-sample rate), clipped back to $[0,1]$; the same realisation is applied to
the training and held-out streams so the comparison isolates each architecture's
intrinsic noise tolerance. Robustness curves are seed-averaged over three bank/noise
seeds; the per-subject significance test at $\sigma=0.4$ is a paired Wilcoxon
signed-rank over the fifteen subjects. Reported metrics are the binary macro-F1, the
pooled false-alarm rate (fraction of not-stress steps called stress), and --- for
completeness, though they do not separate the banks --- the transition-window
macro-F1 (steps within $\pm\SI{20}{\second}$ of a label change) and the onset
detection latency (time to the first $\geq\SI{5}{\second}$ sustained stress call after
each onset).

The cross-corpus replication (\cref{tab:ch5_crosscorpus}, \path{scripts/ch5_noneeg.py})
applies the \emph{same} detector and noise model to the PhysioNet Non-EEG corpus
\cite{Birjandtalab2016} (open access; obtainable with
\path{wfdb.dl_database('noneeg', 'data/noneeg')}). The \SI{8}{\hertz} electrodermal
and temperature record and the \SI{1}{\hertz} heart-rate record are resampled onto a
common timeline and per-subject scaled exactly as the \WESAD{} streams; the phase
annotations map cognitive and emotional stress to the stress class and relaxation to
not-stress, with the physical-stress (exercise) phase excluded.

\section{Control Experiments}\label{app:ch5_controls}

Three controls underpin the claims of \cref{ch:temporal} and are tabulated here.
They are reproduced by \path{scripts/ch5_reservoir.py} (the generic-ESN and coupling
controls) and \path{scripts/ch5_onset.py} (the classical-filter control); each
significance test reports, alongside the paired Wilcoxon $p$, the matched-pairs
rank-biserial effect size $r_{\mathrm{rb}}\in[-1,1]$, since the Wilcoxon $p$ floors
at $2\times10^{-3}$ for the ten-seed comparisons.

\paragraph{Classical-filter control.} \Cref{tab:ch5_filter} is the control behind the
deployment-robustness reframing of \cref{sec:ch5_robust}: an ordinary exponential
moving average of the raw channels, fed to the same linear read-out with no
reservoir, recovers the noise robustness, so the robustness is the generic property
of a fading-memory low-pass rather than anything specific to the device or its
nonlinearity. The device's contribution is to \emph{be} that filter, passively and at
the budget of \cref{app:ch5_deploy}. The cross-corpus and wrist robustness results
(\cref{tab:ch5_crosscorpus,tab:ch5_wrist}) share this character: there too the gain
is the low-pass advantage of temporal memory over an instantaneous read.

\paragraph{Coupling-sensitivity control.} \Cref{tab:ch5_coupling} bounds the
composition assumption of \cref{sec:ch5_model}: each node's retention time is
rescaled with its own write-nonlinearity exponent by a factor $e^{\kappa z}$ ($z$ the
standardised exponent), modelling the drive--retention coupling the separate-amplitude
measurement cannot exclude. The heterogeneous bank stays above the homogeneous one for
every coupling strength swept, so the heterogeneity conclusion does not rest on the
assumption being exact.

\paragraph{Chance baselines.} Under the streaming three-class protocol of
\cref{sec:ch5_wesad}, a majority-class predictor scores macro-F1 $0.23$ and a
stratified-random predictor $0.33$ (leave-one-subject-out, per step), so the
reservoir's $0.758$ is well above chance. Significance of the bank comparisons is
assessed across the fifteen independent held-out subjects, so within-subject temporal
autocorrelation does not inflate it.

\section{Deployment Envelope}\label{app:ch5_deploy}

\Cref{tab:ch5_deployment} collects the operating budget quoted in
\cref{sec:ch5_deploy} (\path{scripts/ch5_deployment.py}). The trainable-parameter
count is exact: the reservoir dynamics are fixed physical devices, and the read-out
is a single $(N{+}1)\times K$ ridge-regression weight matrix. The energy figures are
order-of-magnitude estimates built from the \emph{measured} proof-of-concept device of
\cref{ch:poc} (\cref{app:ch2_si}): an energy per write event of $\approx\SI{50}{\nano\joule}$
(a \SI{1}{\volt}, \SI{0.1}{\second} pulse), equivalently $\approx\SI{6}{\femto\joule\per\micro\metre\squared}$
over the \SI{0.0825}{\square\centi\metre} junction (the two agree to within $1\%$,
$\SI{6}{\femto\joule\per\micro\metre\squared}\times\SI{0.0825}{\square\centi\metre}\approx\SI{49.5}{\nano\joule}$).
The estimate conservatively charges each node one full write event per
one-second update; the sub-threshold, state-preserving read at \SI{0.5}{\volt} is
cheaper, and these are unoptimised large-area figures, so the absolute energy should
be read as an upper bound that integration would lower. The read-out compute is
$N\,K$ multiply--accumulates per step at an assumed \SI{1}{\pico\joule} per operation.
