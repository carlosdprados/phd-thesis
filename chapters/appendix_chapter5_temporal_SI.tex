\chapter{Supporting Information for Chapter~\ref*{ch:temporal}}\label{app:ch5_si}

This appendix documents how the behavioural-model parameter cards of
\cref{sec:ch5_model} are sourced from the measured archive, and lists the
simulation and read-out settings used across the three evidence tiers. Together
with the per-cell tables of \cref{app:ch4_celltables}, it makes the in-silico
results reproducible without consulting the analysis scripts
(\path{scripts/ch5_figures.py}, \path{scripts/ch5_physio_context.py},
\path{scripts/ch5_onset.py}, \path{scripts/ch5_deployment.py},
\path{scripts/ch5_noneeg.py}, \path{scripts/ch5_case.py}).

\section{Parameter-Card Sourcing}\label{app:ch5_cards}

Each replicated composition cell is reduced to a parameter card
$(t_{1/2,c},\beta_c,\alpha_c,R_{\mathrm{peak},c},N_{\mathrm{peak},c})$ that drives one reservoir node type. The values are taken directly from the comparative summaries, with no free re-tuning:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the fading-memory law $\lambda_c(\Delta t)=\exp[-(\Delta t/\tau_c)^{\beta_c}]$ is anchored to the model-free cell-median $t_{1/2,c}$ in \cref{tab:ch4_decay_by_cell}. Where $\beta_c$ is identified, $\tau_c=t_{1/2,c}/(\ln 2)^{1/\beta_c}$; otherwise $\beta_c=1$ and $\tau_c=t_{1/2,c}/\ln 2$;
  \item the write envelope uses the per-cell growth exponent, peak enhancement, and pulse count at the peak from \cref{tab:ch4_pulses_by_cell}; it is normalised to $G/G_0=1$ at zero effective pulses and capped at the measured peak;
  \item the injected device-to-device jitter acts only on the timescale and uses the screened substrate-level within-cell scatter in \cref{app:ch5_scatter}. No common jitter is assumed for $\alpha$ or $\beta$.
\end{itemize}
The heterogeneous banks of \cref{sec:ch5_benchmarks,sec:ch5_physio} span the
replicated \PEO{}/\LiTf{} composition cells of \cref{tab:ch4_decay_by_cell}
($\tau$ from $\approx 4$ to $\approx 30$\,s); the unreplicated \PEO{}~0.15 cell is excluded. Homogeneous controls are jittered copies of the lead cell \PEO{}~0.3/0.09 alone.

\section{Simulation and Read-out Settings}\label{app:ch5_settings}

\Cref{tab:ch5_settings} collects the settings used throughout
\cref{ch:temporal}. All read-outs are a single linear ridge regression; no
nonlinear decoder is used, so any demonstrated computation is attributable to the
device nodes rather than the read-out. The read-out ridge penalty
($\lambda=10^{-3}$), the causal label-smoothing horizon (\SI{15}{\second}), the bank
size $N$, and the reservoir step $\Delta t$ are fixed \emph{a priori} and held
identical across all banks and all held-out subjects; none is selected on the
held-out subject, so the leave-one-subject-out protocol carries no hyperparameter
leakage.

\section{Measured Device-to-Device Scatter}\label{app:ch5_scatter}

The robustness analysis of \cref{sec:ch5_deploy} (\cref{fig:ch5_robustness}) injects variation as multiplicative log-normal jitter on each node's timescale, \(\tau_i=\tau\,e^{\varepsilon}\) with \(\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\). Because \cref{ch:comparative} defines $t_{1/2}$ as the robust primary timescale, the matching estimate is the within-cell spread of \(\ln t_{1/2}\), after junctions have been collapsed to one median per fabricated substrate.

Across the eight replicated cells with finite estimates ($23$ substrates), the median cell standard deviation is \(\sigma(\ln t_{1/2})=\num{0.2637}\); individual cell estimates range from \num{0.0894} to \num{1.0179}. The median is used as the default jitter, and the range motivates the sensitivity sweep. The value is descriptive rather than a precise population parameter because each cell contains few substrates and batch effects cannot be eliminated. A secondary audit of the older, unscreened instrument-side exponential fits gives \(\sigma(\ln\tau)=\num{0.846}\) within cells ($n=27$) and is retained only as an upper stress-test reference. Pooling the entire composition sweep gives \num{1.59} but is invalid as a within-cell estimate because it includes the deliberately designed composition differences. The audit is reproduced by \texttt{scripts/ch5\_scatter\_audit.py}.

\section{Continuous-Monitoring Stress Test}\label{app:ch5_robust}

The deployment-robustness result of \cref{sec:ch5_robust} (\path{scripts/ch5_onset.py})
is a continuous binary stress detector: at every $\Delta t = \SI{1}{\second}$ step the
read-out predicts stress (\WESAD{} label~2) versus not-stress (baseline and amusement
pooled), leave-one-subject-out, with the same single linear ridge read-out and
$15$-second causal label smoothing used in the streaming task. Four matched banks of
$N=24$ nodes are compared on identical input masks: the instantaneous input (no
memory), a memoryless bank (leak set to zero, dimensionality only), the homogeneous
lead-only bank, and the heterogeneous composition bank.

Sensor corruption is modelled by adding, to the per-subject scaled streams
(channels in $[0,1]$), independent Gaussian noise of standard deviation $\sigma$ at
every sample, plus sparse motion-artefact bursts (short windows of amplified noise at
a $1\%$ per-sample rate), clipped back to $[0,1]$; the same realisation is applied to
the training and held-out streams so the comparison isolates each architecture's
intrinsic noise tolerance. Robustness curves are seed-averaged over three bank/noise
seeds; the per-subject significance test at $\sigma=0.4$ is a paired Wilcoxon
signed-rank over the fifteen subjects. Reported metrics are the binary macro-F1, the
pooled false-alarm rate (fraction of not-stress steps called stress), and, for
completeness (though they do not separate the banks), the transition-window
macro-F1 (steps within $\pm\SI{20}{\second}$ of a label change) and the onset
detection latency (time to the first $\geq\SI{5}{\second}$ sustained stress call after
each onset).

The cross-corpus replication (\cref{tab:ch5_crosscorpus}, \path{scripts/ch5_noneeg.py})
applies the \emph{same} detector and noise model to the PhysioNet Non-EEG corpus
\cite{Birjandtalab2016} (open access; obtainable with
\path{wfdb.dl_database('noneeg', 'data/noneeg')}). The \SI{8}{\hertz} electrodermal
and temperature record and the \SI{1}{\hertz} heart-rate record are resampled onto a
common timeline and per-subject scaled exactly as the \WESAD{} streams; the phase
annotations map cognitive and emotional stress to the stress class and relaxation to
not-stress, with the physical-stress (exercise) phase excluded.

\paragraph{Real-motion corruption.} To confirm the robustness is not an artefact of the
Gaussian noise model, the corruption is also driven by the \emph{measured} chest
accelerometer (\path{add_motion_noise}, \path{load_motion} in the scripts). A per-step
motion index is the magnitude of the gravity-removed acceleration, RMS-smoothed over
\SI{1}{\second} and robustly scaled to $[0,1]$ per subject; the injected noise standard
deviation at each step is $\text{scale}\times(0.05 + \text{motion})$, with an amplified
artefact burst where the index exceeds $0.6$, so corruption lands where the wearer
actually moves. Across a corruption scale of $0$ to $0.8$ the instantaneous detector
falls from macro-F1 $0.914$ to $0.898$ while the memory bank holds near $0.92$; at the
strongest gating the memory-minus-instantaneous advantage is $+0.032$ (paired Wilcoxon
$p=0.04$, $r_{\mathrm{rb}}=+0.6$) and the single-$\tau$ moving average matches it
($+0.036$). The advantage is smaller than under the synthetic injection because the
chest strap is mechanically stable and the protocol largely seated; the wrist site
(\cref{tab:ch5_wrist}) is where motion artefact dominates.

\section{Control Experiments}\label{app:ch5_controls}

Three controls underpin the claims of \cref{ch:temporal} and are tabulated here.
They are reproduced by \path{scripts/ch5_reservoir.py} (the generic-ESN and coupling
controls) and \path{scripts/ch5_onset.py} (the classical-filter control). For
seed-only controls, matched means, standard deviations, effect differences, and
sign consistency describe algorithmic sensitivity; no population-level $p$-value is
assigned. Paired Wilcoxon tests and rank-biserial effects are retained only where the
pairs are independent held-out subjects.

\paragraph{Classical-filter control.} \Cref{tab:ch5_filter} is the control behind the
deployment-robustness reframing of \cref{sec:ch5_robust}: an ordinary exponential
moving average of the raw channels, fed to the same linear read-out with no
reservoir, recovers the noise robustness, so the robustness is the generic property
of a fading-memory low-pass rather than anything specific to the device or its
nonlinearity. The device's contribution is to \emph{be} that filter, passively and at
the budget of \cref{app:ch5_deploy}. The cross-corpus and wrist robustness results
(\cref{tab:ch5_crosscorpus,tab:ch5_wrist}) share this character: there too the gain
is the low-pass advantage of temporal memory over an instantaneous read.

\paragraph{Coupling-sensitivity control.} \Cref{tab:ch5_coupling} bounds the
composition assumption of \cref{sec:ch5_model}: each node's retention time is
rescaled with its own write-nonlinearity exponent by a factor $e^{\kappa z}$ ($z$ the
standardised exponent), modelling the drive--retention coupling the separate-amplitude
measurement cannot exclude. Mean capacity remains slightly higher in the heterogeneous bank across the sweep, but the difference is small and not seed-consistent enough to support an inferential robustness claim. The sweep is then \emph{anchored to data}. The gold/\TMPE{}
negative-polarity corpus of \cref{ch:comparative} supplies the only direct measurement
of this coupling: across $28$ single-run potentiation\,$+$\,relaxation traces the
write-depth and the forgetting time are rank-correlated at $\rho=+0.46$ ($p=0.015$;
$95\%$ CI $0.10$--$0.71$ by Fisher's $z$). Calibrating $\kappa$ so the simulated
across-node write--retention correlation matches that figure fixes the data-anchored
operating point. The nominal composed cards already carry an across-node rank correlation of about $\rho=+0.57$, so the central external estimate maps to $\kappa^{\star}=0$; the upper confidence limit maps to about $+0.31$. The heterogeneous-to-homogeneous ratio is $1.08$ at both points, and the sweep spans only $1.04$--$1.09$. The figure is transferred as an order-of-magnitude anchor only -- a
different host, electrode, and polarity -- but it converts the bound from an arbitrary
range into a data-referenced point and independently confirms the \emph{sign} of the
coupling the composed model assumes. The calibration ($\kappa$-for-$\rho$ bisection)
is reproduced by \path{scripts/ch5_reservoir.py}.

\paragraph{Chance baselines.} Under the streaming three-class protocol of
\cref{sec:ch5_wesad}, a majority-class predictor scores macro-F1 $0.23$ and a
stratified-random predictor $0.33$ (leave-one-subject-out, per step), so the
reservoir's $0.758$ is well above chance. Significance of the bank comparisons is
assessed across the fifteen independent held-out subjects, so within-subject temporal
autocorrelation does not inflate it.

\paragraph{Drive-boosted tonic-node extension.} The minutes-scale tonic gap of
\cref{fig:ch5_tau_coverage} is probed by extending the heterogeneous bank with
drive-boosted nodes at $\tau = 120$ and \SI{300}{\second}, a labelled
extrapolation of the drive-amplitude variable, copying the lead cell's write
nonlinearity and stretch and overriding only $\tau$ (\path{tonic_cards} in
\path{scripts/ch5_model.py}). On memory capacity (DT $=\SI{5}{\second}$, $k$ to $40$)
the extension raises the long-lag tail beyond \SI{75}{\second}
($\mathrm{MC}_{k>15}$ from $0.20$ to $0.91$, positive in all ten seed pairs) and raises total
capacity from $4.52$ to $5.03$. On the physiological-context reconstruction, with the
target delays extended to $90$ and \SI{120}{\second} so the genuinely tonic context is
present, the slow-band held-out $R^2$ rises from $0.672$ to $0.690$
($+0.019$, positive in all ten seed pairs) and the overall
$R^2$ by $+0.009$ (\path{tonic_extension} in \path{scripts/ch5_physio_context.py}). The
extension is not part of the measured composition banks anywhere else in the chapter.

\section{Deployment Envelope}\label{app:ch5_deploy}

\Cref{tab:ch5_deployment} collects the operating budget quoted in
\cref{sec:ch5_deploy} (\path{scripts/ch5_deployment.py}). The trainable-parameter
count is exact: the reservoir dynamics are fixed physical devices, and the read-out
is a single $(N{+}1)\times K$ ridge-regression weight matrix. The energy figures are
order-of-magnitude estimates built from the \emph{measured} proof-of-concept device of
\cref{ch:poc} (\cref{app:ch2_si}): an energy per write event of $\approx\SI{50}{\nano\joule}$
(a \SI{1}{\volt}, \SI{0.1}{\second} pulse), equivalently $\approx\SI{6}{\femto\joule\per\micro\metre\squared}$
over the \SI{0.0825}{\square\centi\metre} junction (the two agree to within $1\%$,
$\SI{6}{\femto\joule\per\micro\metre\squared}\times\SI{0.0825}{\square\centi\metre}\approx\SI{49.5}{\nano\joule}$).
The estimate conservatively charges each node one full write event per
one-second update; the sub-threshold, state-preserving read at \SI{0.5}{\volt} is
cheaper, and these are unoptimised large-area figures, so the absolute energy should
be read as an upper bound that integration would lower. The read-out compute is
$N\,K$ multiply--accumulates per step at an assumed \SI{1}{\pico\joule} per operation.

\section{Continuous Affect: CASE Protocol and Results}\label{app:ch5_case}

\Cref{tab:ch5_case} gives the full continuous valence/arousal result behind
\cref{sec:ch5_case} (\path{scripts/ch5_case.py}). The \CASE{} corpus
\cite{Sharma2019CASE} (open access; figshare \texttt{10.6084/m9.figshare.8869157})
provides, for thirty subjects, a continuous joystick annotation of valence and
arousal (range $[0.5,9.5]$, \SI{20}{\hertz}) synchronised with eight physiological
channels at \SI{1}{\kilo\hertz}. We use the four slow channels that match the device
band and the \WESAD{} set -- electrodermal activity, respiration, skin temperature,
and a heart rate recovered from the electrocardiogram by the same robust R-peak
detector used for \WESAD{} -- resampled to the $\Delta t=\SI{1}{\second}$ reservoir
cadence over the eight emotional videos (the blue baseline clips are excluded),
per-subject robustly scaled exactly as the \WESAD{} streams. The reservoir state is
reset and a \SI{10}{\second} washout dropped at each video boundary. The read-out is a
single linear ridge regression predicting valence and arousal jointly,
leave-one-subject-out, scored by Lin's concordance correlation coefficient (CCC)
\cite{Lin1989CCC}, $\mathrm{CCC}=2\,\mathrm{cov}(y,\hat y)/[\sigma_y^2+\sigma_{\hat y}^2+(\mu_y-\mu_{\hat y})^2]$.

Two classical controls bound the device. The \emph{EMA} rows replace the reservoir
with an exponential moving average of the raw channels fed to the same read-out: a
single time constant (lead cell), and a bank spread over the device timescale range
(\SIrange{3}{26}{\second}). The \emph{per-channel} device bank gives each node a
single-channel input mask instead of the random multi-channel mask -- the
apples-to-apples device analogue of the linear EMA bank -- so the device-minus-EMA
gap isolates the compressive nonlinearity rather than the mask. Paired Wilcoxon tests
over the thirty held-out subjects (mean CCC per subject, seed-averaged):
$\tau$-spread $-$ single-$\tau$ (EMA) $=+0.176$ ($27/30$, $p=1.6\times10^{-7}$,
$r_{\mathrm{rb}}=+0.94$); memory $-$ instantaneous $=+0.079$ ($24/30$,
$p=1.2\times10^{-4}$); per-channel device $-$ EMA bank $=-0.047$ ($p=6.6\times10^{-3}$);
heterogeneous $-$ homogeneous device $=+0.005$ ($p=0.18$, \textit{ns}).
