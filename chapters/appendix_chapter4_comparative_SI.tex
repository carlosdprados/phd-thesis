\chapter{Supporting Information for Chapter~\ref*{ch:comparative}}\label{app:ch4_si}

This appendix gathers the analysis provenance, the confound audits, the
fit methodology, and the per-composition-cell quantitative tables that underlie
\cref{ch:comparative}. The main chapter retains the result and the figures; the
material here records how the numbers were produced and lists the per-cell values
behind the heatmaps and design-space plots.

\section{Analysis Provenance and Aggregation}\label{app:ch4_provenance}

\paragraph{Data-quality control.} Two screening layers are applied before any
number is reported, both recorded so that the analysis is reproducible.
Individual measurements that are erratic or non-physical are flagged and
excluded. In addition, a per-device visual review of the recorded curves was
carried out; the resulting verdicts --- whether a curve is used as measured,
cleaned by removing identified anomalous points, or discarded --- together with
the exact points retained, are stored in a curation registry and applied
automatically by the analysis scripts, rather than being made ad hoc. The fitted
timescale obtained directly from the instrument-side pipeline was found not to be
robust to such anomalous points, so all timescales reported in \cref{ch:comparative}
are recomputed after this screening. \Cref{tab:ch4_provenance} records the full
raw-to-summary trail underlying these screened descriptors.

\paragraph{The aggregation ladder.} The aggregation is deliberately
conservative. HYST raw points are first reduced to sweep-level features in
\path{DEVICES_HYST_CURVE_INFO.csv}; broken or FILTERED rows are removed; device
summaries are medians across the surviving sweeps and pixels; and
composition-cell values are medians across devices. PULSES and DELAYTIME follow
the same curve/pixel/device/cell logic, but with protocol-specific descriptors:
the pulse curves are summarised by \(\alpha\), peak ratio, and turnover, while the
decay curves are summarised primarily by \(t_{1/2}\), with \(\tau\) and \(\beta\)
retained only when the fit passes the identification thresholds in
\path{scripts/ch4_dynamics_fits.py}. The representative curves in
\cref{fig:ch4_representative} are selected by the figure script from devices near
the relevant cell medians, so they are illustrative examples of the same
summarized population rather than hand-picked evidence outside the pipeline.

\paragraph{Stretched-exponential identification.} The depotentiation decays are
fitted with the Kohlrausch form of \cref{eq:ch4_kohlrausch}. The
eight-to-thirteen-point decay curves often under-constrain \(\beta\) and \(\tau\)
jointly, so the fitted \(\tau\) is reported only where the fit is well identified,
and the model-free half-enhancement time \(t_{1/2}\) is used as the primary
timescale; where both are available they agree (\cref{tab:ch4_decay_by_cell}).
The stretch exponent is constrained to the physical Kohlrausch range
\(0<\beta\le1\) of \cref{eq:ch4_kohlrausch}: an unconstrained fit occasionally
returns \(\beta>1\) (a \emph{compressed} exponential, which does not correspond
to a distribution of activation barriers), and such cells are instead pinned at
\(\beta=1\), i.e.\ a near-undispersed simple-exponential relaxation, rather than
reported as a spurious stretched fit. Fitted \(\beta\) values then lie
predominantly in \numrange{0.6}{0.9}, i.e.\ genuinely stretched relaxation
consistent with the heterogeneous distribution of activation barriers invoked in
\cref{ch:poc}, with the most ion-rich cells (high \PEO{}, high salt) approaching
\(\beta=1\) --- a narrower barrier distribution, as expected when ionic transport
dominates. The intra-device disorder implied by the stretch and the
device-to-device heterogeneity \(\sigma(\ln\tau)\) of \cref{app:ch5_scatter} are
related rather than independent: the relaxation-time spread implied by the median
stretch is comparable in magnitude to the measured inter-device scatter
(\cref{app:ch4_resource}), so the same barrier-distribution width underlies both.

\section{Film-Thickness and Fabrication Confound Audits}\label{app:ch4_confounds}

\paragraph{Film thickness is a controlled covariate.} In most batches, the
spin-coating speed was raised for higher-\PEO{} cells to partly equalise
thickness (spanning \SIrange{1500}{3500}{rpm}). The compensation was deliberate
but incomplete: median film thickness still rises from
\(\approx\SI{227}{\nano\meter}\) at \PEO{}~\num{0.3} to
\(\approx\SI{298}{\nano\meter}\) at \PEO{}~\num{1.2}, giving a Pearson
\(r\approx0.68\) between \PEO{} fraction and thickness
(\cref{fig:ch4_thickness_control}).
Four observations show that thickness is nonetheless not the operative variable:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The reported metrics are dimensionless ratios and timescales (on--off ratio, log--log slope, relaxation time), in which an absolute series-resistance or geometric effect of thickness cancels.
  \item The activation voltage is flat (\SIrange{2.2}{2.4}{\volt}, \(r\approx-0.06\) against thickness) across a \(2.6\times\) thickness range, so the switching threshold is an intrinsic ionic property rather than a bulk-field effect that would scale with film thickness.
  \item Within the single lead cell (\PEO{}~\num{0.3}/salt~\num{0.09}) a \(38\%\) device-to-device thickness spread (\SIrange{196}{271}{\nano\meter}) leaves the fading-memory time essentially unchanged (\(t_{1/2}\approx\SIrange{18}{22}{\second}\)).
  \item Most directly, once composition is held fixed the thickness correlation with \(\log t_{1/2}\) vanishes (partial \(r\approx+0.05\)), while the \PEO{} effect survives after controlling for thickness (partial \(r\approx-0.42\)); the same holds for the potentiation metrics.
\end{enumerate}
Thickness covaries with \PEO{} as a downstream consequence of polymer loading, but
the dynamics track composition. The full join and statistics are reproduced by
\path{scripts/thickness_rpm_audit.py} and
\path{handouts/14_thickness_rpm_confound_audit.md}.

\paragraph{Other fabrication variables are held constant or crossed.} The full
per-device fabrication record was audited to confirm that the composition trends
are not driven by some other processing variable. Across the silver composition
grid the principal process variables are held \emph{constant}: the annealing
temperature (\SI{75}{\celsius}, no second stage), the electrode (\SI{100}{\nano\meter}
silver, evaporated by the same operator), the semiconductor loading, the solvent,
and --- which matters for a hygroscopic polyether --- storage \emph{and} measurement
under a glovebox atmosphere, so ambient humidity is controlled out. The variables
that do change across the grid (fabrication batch and date, the operator who
prepared the solutions, the semiconductor lot, and the device age at measurement)
are \emph{crossed} with composition rather than confounded with it: each
composition cell is populated from several independent batches, and within any
single batch every composition level was fabricated together and measured on the
same day. The composition trends are accordingly reproduced \emph{within}
individual single-day batches --- including one in which the spin speed was held
fixed across all compositions, so that the trend appears with no thickness
compensation at all. The full column-by-column audit is recorded in
\texttt{handouts/15\_fabrication\_confound\_audit.md} and reproduced by
\texttt{scripts/fabrication\_confound\_audit.py}.

\section{Impedance: Equivalent Circuit and Per-Cell Values}\label{app:ch4_eis_detail}

\paragraph{Equivalent-circuit model.} Beyond the model-free Nyquist-apex
resistance used in \cref{sec:ch4_eis}, each spectrum was also summarised by the
bulk ionic resistance \(R_{\mathrm{ion}}\) of an equivalent circuit adapted from
the polymer light-emitting electrochemical cell analysis of Munar \textit{et
al.}~\cite{Munar2012}, in which a series cable term is placed in parallel with
(i) the semiconductor electronic branch (effectively open at \SI{0}{\volt}),
(ii) a geometric constant-phase element, and (iii) an ionic branch of two
interfacial double layers in series with \(R_{\mathrm{ion}}\). Two cautions
apply. First, the archive holds two automated fits of this circuit; the
freely-fitted one (with per-parameter error estimates) tracks composition
appreciably better than a more tightly bounded variant, and only the former is
used. Second, per-spectrum circuit fits are genuinely ill-conditioned --- an
independent refit confirms that several do not converge robustly --- so
\(R_{\mathrm{ion}}\) is reported only as corroboration of the model-free quantity,
never as a precise number. Both observables nonetheless fall steeply and
monotonically with \PEO{} fraction (\cref{tab:ch4_eis_by_cell}): the model-free
\(Z_{\mathrm{real}}^{\mathrm{apex}}\) at rank correlation \(-0.83\) against \PEO{}
on cell medians, the fitted \(R_{\mathrm{ion}}\) at \(-0.84\).

\section{Significance of the Composition Gradient}\label{app:ch4_gradient}

The composition trends of \cref{sec:ch4_composition} are reported in the main text
as cell medians. This section attaches a significance test and a non-parametric
uncertainty to them, on the replicated Ag/\ce{Li} \SY{}/\PEO{}/\LiTf{} series. For
each descriptor we compute the Spearman rank correlation with the \PEO{} fraction
across individual devices, with a two-sided permutation \(p\)-value
(\num{20000} relabellings; \cref{tab:ch4_gradient}). The fading-memory time
\(t_{1/2}\) --- the descriptor that matters for \cref{ch:temporal} --- carries a
clearly significant \PEO{} gradient (\(\rho=-0.52\), \(p=0.009\), \(n=25\)). The
remaining descriptors fall in the same direction but are only marginal at this
sample size (\(p\approx0.06\text{--}0.07\)): the per-device gradient is genuinely
weaker and noisier than the cell medians alone suggest, and we report it as
suggestive rather than established. This is the honest counterpart to the median
heatmaps --- the timescale trend is statistically secure, the window and
potentiation trends are indicative.

\section{Absolute Conductance Tests the Dilution Picture}\label{app:ch4_percolation}

The working mechanism of \cref{sec:ch4_discussion} initially read the window
contraction at high \PEO{} as a \emph{dilution} of the \SY{}
electronic-percolation network. That picture makes a direct, falsifiable
prediction on a quantity the peak-normalised dynamics discard: if added
ion-transport polymer simply interrupted the electronic network, the
\emph{absolute} on-state conductance should \emph{fall} with \PEO{}. It does the
opposite. The on-state conductance (HYST conductance at the sweep maximum, device
medians, \(n=47\) Ag series devices) \emph{rises} steeply and monotonically with
\PEO{} --- Spearman \(\rho=+0.65\) (\(p<10^{-4}\)), a log--log slope of
\(\approx+3.6\), and more than three orders of magnitude from \PEO{}~\num{0.15} to
\num{1.2} (\cref{fig:ch4_percolation}). Because film thickness rises with \PEO{}
(\cref{app:ch4_confounds}), a geometric effect would push the conductance the
other way, so the rise is if anything understated.

The dilution reading is therefore wrong in its literal form, and the corrected
mechanism is the one carried in the revised \cref{sec:ch4_discussion}: the
ion-rich phase contributes a parallel, weakly switchable conduction path that
\emph{raises the conductance floor}. Since the on--off ratio nonetheless falls
with \PEO{}, the off-state conductance must rise even faster than the on-state, so
the window contracts not because the network is diluted but because its baseline
is lifted toward the on-level. The fading-memory and potentiation trends are
unaffected by this correction --- they were always read in terms of ionic
relaxation, not electronic percolation.

\section{Reconciling the Impedance and Fading-Memory Timescales}\label{app:ch4_timescale}

Section~\ref{sec:ch4_eis} notes that the EIS resistance \emph{scale} tracks the
fading-memory time across composition, while the small-signal apex \emph{frequency}
does not: the Nyquist-apex \(RC\) time is
\(\tau_{RC}=1/(2\pi f_{\mathrm{apex}})\approx\SI{0.12}{\second}\) (Ag series,
\SI{0}{\volt} DC, \(n=26\)), more than an order of magnitude shorter than the
seconds-long \(t_{1/2}\). This section shows the gap is not a contradiction but a
small-signal/large-signal capacitance ratio, so the two land at the same
\emph{magnitude}.

The apex \(RC\) is set by the geometric capacitance
\(C_{\mathrm{geo}}=1/(2\pi f_{\mathrm{apex}}Z^{\mathrm{apex}}_{\mathrm{real}})\).
The forgetting of a \emph{written} state, by contrast, is the relaxation of a
displaced ionic profile and is governed by the much larger chemical/double-layer
capacitance \(C_{\mathrm{chem}}\) of the ion-rich film. Measuring
\(C_{\mathrm{chem}}\) independently from the low-frequency branch of the same
spectra (\(C_{\mathrm{lf}}=-1/(2\pi f Z_{\mathrm{imag}})\) at the lowest measured
frequency) gives a capacitance enhancement
\(C_{\mathrm{lf}}/C_{\mathrm{geo}}\approx33\) (median, \(n=26\)) --- and since the
lowest measured frequency does not reach the full DC plateau, this is a
\emph{lower bound}. The predicted large-signal relaxation is then
\(\tau_{\mathrm{large}}\approx\tau_{RC}\,(C_{\mathrm{chem}}/C_{\mathrm{geo}})
\gtrsim\SI{3.9}{\second}\), squarely inside the observed \(t_{1/2}\) range of
\SIrange{2}{22}{\second} and within a factor of two of the series median
(\SI{6.6}{\second}). The independently measured capacitance enhancement thus
accounts for the timescale separation quantitatively, upgrading the impedance
corroboration of \cref{sec:ch4_eis} from same-direction to same-magnitude.

\section{Within-Device Reproducibility}\label{app:ch4_reproducibility}

A physical reservoir requires elements that are diverse \emph{across} the bank but
\emph{individually reproducible}. The chapter documents the diversity at length;
this section quantifies the reproducibility. The fading-memory protocol measured a
single junction per device, so a population-level repeat-\emph{decay}
reproducibility cannot be estimated --- only one series device carries two
junctions, and there the model-free \(t_{1/2}\) agrees to
\(\sigma(\ln t_{1/2})\approx0.19\); broader repeat-decay statistics are a stated
gap (\cref{sec:ch4_limitations}). The steady-state switching window, however, is
recorded over several sweeps per junction (median \(\approx5\)), giving a
genuine population-level within-device reproducibility: the sweep-to-sweep scatter
of the on--off ratio is \(\sigma(\ln R_{\mathrm{on/off}})\approx0.14\) across
\num{165} series junctions, i.e.\ each junction reproduces its switching window to
\(\approx\SI{15}{\percent}\). This within-device scatter is far smaller than the
across-composition span the bank exploits (\(\Delta\ln t_{1/2}\approx2.1\),
\cref{app:ch4_resource}), so a device's behaviour is a stable signature relative
to the diversity the substrate spans --- the premise \cref{ch:temporal} relies on.

\section{Heterogeneity as a Computational Resource}\label{app:ch4_resource}

The chapter treats the spread of fading-memory times as a resource rather than
noise. This section makes the claim a number. The cell-median \(t_{1/2}\) spans
\(\Delta\ln t_{1/2}\approx2.1\) across the composition series (from
\(\approx\SI{2.7}{\second}\) to \(\approx\SI{22}{\second}\)), while the
device-to-device scatter \emph{within} a cell is
\(\sigma_{\mathrm{within}}(\ln t_{1/2})\approx0.29\) on the robust model-free
timescale, rising to \(\approx0.85\) on the fit-noisier single-exponential
\(\tau\) cited in \cref{app:ch5_scatter}. Taking a two-\(\sigma\) separation as the
criterion for two timescales to be distinguishable, the bank provides
\(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=1+\Delta\ln t_{1/2}/(2\sigma_{\mathrm{within}})\approx2.2\)
(using the conservative \(\sigma=0.85\)) to \(\approx4.6\) (using the model-free
\(\sigma=0.29\)) resolvably distinct memory bands selectable by composition. The
resource is therefore modest but real: a handful of cleanly separated timescales,
not a continuum, which is why \cref{ch:temporal} assembles a \emph{bank} of
compositions rather than tuning a single optimum.

\paragraph{Single-device and inter-device disorder are linked.} The stretched
exponent provides an independent read on dispersion: the median single-device
stretch corresponds to a barrier-distribution width \(1-\beta\approx0.36\), of the
same order as the within-cell device-to-device \(\sigma(\ln t_{1/2})\). The
disorder seen \emph{within} one device's relaxation and the spread \emph{between}
sibling devices are thus comparable in magnitude, consistent with both arising
from the same heterogeneous distribution of local activation barriers rather than
from independent causes.
