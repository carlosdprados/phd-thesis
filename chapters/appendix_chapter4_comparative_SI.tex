\chapter{Supporting Information for Chapter~\ref*{ch:comparative}}\label{app:ch4_si}

This appendix gathers the analysis traceability, the confound audits, the
fit methodology, and the per-composition-cell quantitative tables that underlie
\cref{ch:comparative}. The main chapter retains the result and the figures; the
material here records how the numbers were produced and lists the per-cell values
behind the heatmaps and design-space plots.

\section{Analysis Traceability and Aggregation}\label{app:ch4_provenance}

\paragraph{Data-quality control.} Two screening layers are applied before any
number is reported, both recorded so that the analysis is reproducible.
Individual measurements that are erratic or non-physical are flagged and
excluded, including the archive's “Y/N” broken flags after normalisation. In
addition, a visual review of the recorded curves was carried out; the resulting
verdicts, whether a curve is used as measured, cleaned by removing identified
anomalous points, or discarded, together with the exact points retained, are
stored under the full device--measurement--day--pixel key in a curation registry.
The most specific matching verdict is applied automatically by the analysis
scripts, instead of being made ad hoc. The fitted
timescale obtained directly from the instrument-side pipeline was found not to be
robust to such anomalous points, so all timescales reported in \cref{ch:comparative}
are recomputed after this screening. \Cref{tab:ch4_provenance} records the full
raw-to-summary trail underlying these screened descriptors.

\paragraph{The aggregation ladder.} The aggregation is deliberately
conservative. HYST raw points are first reduced to sweep-level features in
\path{DEVICES_HYST_CURVE_INFO.csv}; broken or FILTERED rows are removed; surviving
sweeps are reduced first to junction medians and then to one median per fabricated
substrate; composition-cell values are medians across substrates. PULSES and
DELAYTIME follow the same curve/junction/substrate/cell logic, but with
protocol-specific descriptors:
the pulse curves are summarised by \(\alpha\), peak ratio, and turnover, while the
decay curves are summarised primarily by \(t_{1/2}\), with \(\tau\) and \(\beta\)
retained only when the fit passes the identification thresholds in
\path{scripts/ch4_dynamics_fits.py}. The representative curves in
\cref{fig:ch4_representative} are selected by the figure script from substrates near
the relevant cell medians, so they are illustrative examples of the same
summarized population, not hand-picked evidence outside the pipeline.

\paragraph{Stretched-exponential identification.} The depotentiation decays are
fitted with the Kohlrausch form of \cref{eq:ch4_kohlrausch}. The
eight-to-thirteen-point decay curves often under-constrain \(\beta\) and \(\tau\)
jointly, so the fitted \(\tau\) is reported only where the fit is well identified,
and the model-free half-enhancement time \(t_{1/2}\) is used as the primary
timescale; where both are available they agree (\cref{tab:ch4_decay_by_cell}).
The stretch exponent is constrained to the physical Kohlrausch range
\(0<\beta\le1\) of \cref{eq:ch4_kohlrausch}: an unconstrained fit occasionally
returns \(\beta>1\) (a \emph{compressed} exponential, which does not correspond
to a distribution of activation barriers), and such cells are instead pinned at
\(\beta=1\), i.e.\ a near-undispersed simple-exponential relaxation, not
reported as a spurious stretched fit. Fitted \(\beta\) values then lie
predominantly in \numrange{0.6}{0.9}, i.e.\ genuinely stretched relaxation
consistent with a distribution of relaxation times. The most ion-rich cells
(high \PEO{}, high salt) approach \(\beta=1\), but the sparse fits do not identify
a unique microscopic origin for that pattern. The dimensionless quantity
\(1-\beta\) describes within-curve stretching under this model; it is not
commensurate with the between-substrate statistic \(\sigma(\ln t_{1/2})\).
Their numerical similarity is therefore reported only as a descriptive
comparison (\cref{app:ch4_resource}), not as evidence that one barrier-width
parameter causes both.

\section{Film-Thickness and Fabrication Confound Audits}\label{app:ch4_confounds}

\paragraph{Film thickness as a measured covariate.} In most batches, the
spin-coating speed was raised for higher-\PEO{} cells to partly equalise
thickness (spanning \SIrange{1500}{3500}{rpm}). The compensation was deliberate
but incomplete: median film thickness still rises from
\(\approx\SI{227}{\nano\meter}\) at \PEO{}~\num{0.3} to
\(\approx\SI{298}{\nano\meter}\) at \PEO{}~\num{1.2}, giving a Pearson
\(r\approx0.65\) between \(r_{\PEO{}}\) and thickness
(\cref{fig:ch4_thickness_control}).
Four observations constrain, but do not eliminate, a thickness contribution:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Conductance ratios reduce sensitivity to absolute geometry, but the timescales need not be thickness-independent; this is why a direct covariate audit is required.
  \item The activation voltage has no resolved thickness trend (\(r\approx-0.06\)) across a \(2.6\times\) thickness range. This is inconsistent with simple inverse-thickness threshold scaling, but it does not prove an intrinsic mechanism.
  \item Within the single lead cell (\PEO{}~\num{0.3}/salt~\num{0.09}) a \(38\%\) device-to-device thickness spread (\SIrange{196}{271}{\nano\meter}) leaves the fading-memory time essentially unchanged (\(t_{1/2}\approx\SIrange{18}{22}{\second}\)).
  \item In the \num{23} substrates with \(t_{1/2}\), the partial correlation of thickness with \(\log t_{1/2}\) at fixed \PEO{} is \(+0.28\), while the \PEO{} association at fixed thickness remains \(-0.51\).
\end{enumerate}
The composition association therefore persists after adjustment for measured
thickness, but this sample does not exclude a secondary thickness contribution.
The full join and statistics are reproduced by
\path{scripts/thickness_rpm_audit.py} and
\path{handouts/14_thickness_rpm_confound_audit.md}.

\paragraph{Other fabrication variables are held constant or crossed.} The full
per-device fabrication record was audited to confirm that the composition trends
are not driven by some other processing variable. Across the silver composition
grid the principal process variables are held \emph{constant}: the annealing
temperature (\SI{75}{\celsius}, no second stage), the electrode (\SI{100}{\nano\meter}
silver, evaporated by the same operator), the semiconductor loading, the solvent,
and, which matters for a hygroscopic polyether, storage \emph{and} measurement
under a glovebox atmosphere, so ambient humidity is controlled out. The variables
that do change across the grid (fabrication batch and date, the operator who
prepared the solutions, the semiconductor lot, and the device age at measurement)
are \emph{crossed} with composition, not confounded with it: each
composition cell is populated from several independent batches, and within any
single batch every composition level was fabricated together and measured on the
same day. The composition trends are accordingly reproduced \emph{within}
individual single-day batches, including one in which the spin speed was held
fixed across all compositions, so that the trend appears with no thickness
compensation at all. The full column-by-column audit is recorded in
\texttt{handouts/15\_fabrication\_confound\_audit.md} and reproduced by
\texttt{scripts/fabrication\_confound\_audit.py}.

\section{Impedance: Equivalent Circuit and Per-Cell Values}\label{app:ch4_eis_detail}

\paragraph{Equivalent-circuit model.} Beyond the model-free Nyquist-apex
resistance used in \cref{sec:ch4_eis}, each spectrum was also summarised by the
bulk ionic resistance \(R_{\mathrm{ion}}\) of an equivalent circuit adapted from
the polymer light-emitting electrochemical cell analysis of Munar \textit{et
al.}~\cite{Munar2012}, in which a series cable term is placed in parallel with
(i) the semiconductor electronic branch (effectively open at \SI{0}{\volt}),
(ii) a geometric constant-phase element, and (iii) an ionic branch of two
interfacial double layers in series with \(R_{\mathrm{ion}}\). Two cautions
apply. First, the archive holds two automated fits of this circuit; the
freely-fitted one (with per-parameter error estimates) tracks composition
appreciably better than a more tightly bounded variant, and only the former is
used. Second, per-spectrum circuit fits are genuinely ill-conditioned, an
independent refit confirms that several do not converge robustly, so
\(R_{\mathrm{ion}}\) is reported only as corroboration of the model-free quantity,
never as a precise number. Both observables nonetheless fall steeply and
monotonically with \(r_{\PEO{}}\) (\cref{tab:ch4_eis_by_cell}): the model-free
\(Z_{\mathrm{real}}^{\mathrm{apex}}\) at rank correlation \(-0.83\) against \(r_{\PEO{}}\)
on cell medians, the fitted \(R_{\mathrm{ion}}\) at \(-0.84\).

\section{The Gold-Electrode Negative-Polarity Dynamics Corpus}\label{app:ch4_gold_dynamics}

\section{The Gold-Electrode Lock-in Frequency-Sweep Corpus}\label{app:ch4_lockin}

This section documents the origin, measurement decode, and quantitative
findings of the lock-in frequency-sweep corpus behind the frequency-domain
results of \cref{sec:ch4_electrode}. Reproduction:
\texttt{scripts/lockin\_au\_tmpe\_audit.py} (filename parser, per-file
metrics, QA panels) and \texttt{scripts/lockin\_au\_tmpe\_findings.py}
(analyses L1--L5); per-file tables in \texttt{handouts/lockin\_manifest.csv},
\texttt{handouts/lockin\_metrics.csv}, and
\texttt{handouts/lockin\_findings\_*.csv}; a full audit record in
\texttt{handouts/29\_lockin\_au\_tmpe\_audit.md}.

\paragraph{Provenance.} The corpus comprises \num{549} lock-in sweeper exports
(plus \num{439} binary siblings) taken between 2025-03-04 and 2025-03-26 on a
single Au/\TMPE{}/triflate batch: the device-age field in every filename
equals the measurement date minus 2025-03-03, so the batch has a single,
self-consistent fabrication (top-electrode) date and is tracked from day 1 to
day 23. Twelve pixels on three substrates were measured, one substrate per
triflate salt (\ce{Li}, \ce{Na}, \ce{K}). The substrates are not catalogued in
the device library, the experimenter's own labels carry an ``unknown''
prefix, but by chemistry, recipe, and date they are most plausibly the
electrode-free UV-Vis trio of 2025-02-26 (\cref{fig:ch4_uvvis}) after Au
evaporation, or unlogged siblings of that batch; this identification is
unproven, the same caveat class as the February campaigns of
\siCompRef{app:ch4_gold_dynamics}.

\paragraph{Measurement and decode.} A Zurich Instruments MFLI drives
\(V_\mathrm{off} + V_\mathrm{pk}\sin(2\pi f t)\) through the junction into an
external series capacitor to ground (\(C_\mathrm{ext}=\SI{4}{\nano\farad}\)
standard; a \SIrange{0.286}{2}{\nano\farad} series on one pixel; a
``\SI{2.5}{\pico\farad}'' day-1/2 configuration with no external capacitor,
loaded by the instrument's \SI{10}{\mega\ohm} input); the voltage at the
junction--capacitor node is demodulated at the drive frequency, and the
transfer ratio is \(|H| = V_\mathrm{out,RMS}/(V_\mathrm{pk}/\sqrt{2})\), the
normalisation used by the corpus' own visualisation script. Sweeps descend
from \SI{500}{\kilo\hertz} to \SI{1}{\hertz} in three captures
(\num{100}+\num{97}+\num{200} points, \(\sim\!\SI{1}{\second}\) per point);
the \num{110} ``500k--1Hz'' files are \emph{derived} merges of the three
captures (produced by the corpus' \texttt{combine\_freq.py}; frequency-sorted,
timer running backwards) and are excluded from all statistics to avoid double
counting. Of the four recorded demodulator channels, demodulator~4 is the
output fundamental used throughout; demodulator~2 duplicates it through a
different demodulator filter; demodulator~3 behaves as a DC monitor of the
output node (magnitude = DC value, sign carried by its phase); demodulator~1
is an auxiliary current channel, unused here. A \SI{50}{\hertz} mains glitch
is visible in most captures and lies on none of the probe frequencies used
below.

\paragraph{L1, divider validation.} On unkK2-L5 (day 3, \SI{0.5}{Vpk},
\SI{10}{\kilo\hertz}), stepping \(C_\mathrm{ext}\) through
\numlist{0.286;0.5;0.667;1;1.3;1.66;2;4}~\si{\nano\farad} moves \(|H|\) from
\num{0.830} to \num{0.423}, and the implied device capacitance
\(C_\mathrm{dev} = C_\mathrm{ext}|H|/(1-|H|)\) stays within
\SIrange{1.4}{2.9}{\nano\farad} (median \SI{2.35}{\nano\farad}). The residual
monotone trend of \(C_\mathrm{dev}\) with \(C_\mathrm{ext}\) is the expected
signature of a lossy (constant-phase-like) dielectric, not an ideal one; the
divider model is therefore taken as validated at the factor level, not as an
exact calibration.

\paragraph{L2, the passive band is linear.} At zero offset and
\(f\gtrsim\SI{10}{\hertz}\) the junction is a passive dielectric:
across the \numrange{0.5}{2.4}~Vpk amplitude series the plateau \(|H|\) at
\SI{1}{\kilo\hertz} and \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz} changes by a median
\SIrange{0.17}{0.29}{\percent} (maximum \SI{8}{\percent}; \num{9}
device-days). The effective capacitance at \SI{1}{\kilo\hertz} is
\SI{2.32}{\nano\farad} (\ce{Li}), \SI{2.93}{\nano\farad} (\ce{Na}), and
\SI{3.19}{\nano\farad} (\ce{K}) by substrate,
\(\approx\)\SIrange{28}{39}{\nano\farad\per\centi\meter\squared}
at the \SI{8.25e-2}{\centi\meter\squared} junction area, i.e.\
\(\varepsilon_r\approx5\)--\(7\) at the \SIrange{150}{190}{\nano\meter}
that profilometry measures on sibling substrates of the same recipe,
with a pixel-to-pixel spread \emph{within} each substrate of
\(\approx\!\SI{1}{\percent}\).

\paragraph{A candidate cation-ordered capacitance.} The capacitance is
itself cation-ordered, \(\ce{K}>\ce{Na}>\ce{Li}\) with a span of
\(\approx\!1.4\times\), two orders of magnitude above the within-substrate
pixel spread, on three substrates fabricated in a single batch. With one
substrate per salt this cannot be fully separated from substrate-level film
variation, but the archive's profilometry of the same recipe (\TMPE{}
\num{0.3}/salt \num{0.09}/\num{3000} rpm;
\texttt{handouts/lockin\_findings\_thickness.csv}) bounds the one plausible
nuisance channel. Explaining the span by thickness alone would require the
\ce{Li} film \(\approx\!\SI{37}{\percent}\) thicker than the \ce{K} film;
the two profiled same-day cation trios differ by only
\SIrange{6}{12}{\percent} across salts, and in the anion-matched 2024-10-29
pair the \ce{K} film is the \emph{thicker} one, the direction that would
weaken, not produce, the observed ordering. Salt-uncorrelated
solution-to-solution scatter does reach \(\approx\!\SI{30}{\percent}\) in
the archive, so a thickness origin is not excluded, merely unprecedented in
cation-correlated form. The ordering is therefore recorded as a
\emph{candidate} cation dielectric effect, an effective permittivity
rising from \ce{Li+} to \ce{K+}, the direction qualitative
coordination-strength reasoning would suggest (the most weakly coordinated
cation the most polarizable), and it is the one cleanly cation-ordered observable
in this archive. It is not in tension with the driven retention, which also
moves with the cation, the silver \TMPE{}/triflate series runs in the same
\ce{K}>\ce{Na}>\ce{Li} direction, but which cannot be fixed to a
host- and anion-independent ordering at one or two devices per cell. The flat
probes (ion association by ATR, the optical gap by UV-Vis) address different
physics (ion pairing, electronic structure) and are expected to be flat, so
there is no contradiction. It is not advanced as demonstrated. The decisive, cheap test
is profilometry on these three substrates, which still exist, followed by
the replicated cation series of \cref{sec:ch4_limitations}. The gain
magnitudes of the next paragraph are deliberately \emph{not} given the same
reading: unlike the capacitance plateau (offset-insensitive, week-stable,
\SI{1}{\percent} uniform), the low-frequency gain is sensitive to
conditioning history and session protocol, both of which differ across the
three substrates.

\paragraph{L3, bias-activated low-frequency gain.}
\Cref{tab:ch4_lockin_gain} gives the day-23 offset series on unkLi2-L3
(\num{11} offsets, \SI{0.25}{Vpk}): the fundamental-frequency transfer at
\SIrange{1}{2}{\hertz} rises monotonically with \(|V_\mathrm{off}|\), from
\(|H|\approx0.5\) unbiased to \(\approx\!18\) at \(\pm\SI{2.5}{\volt}\),
while \(|H|\) at \SI{10}{\hertz} stays near unity and the
\(\geq\!\SI{1}{\kilo\hertz}\) plateau does not move (offset and zero-offset
files give plateau medians \num{0.422} and \num{0.423}). Across the corpus
the prevalence of gain (\(|H|_{2\,\mathrm{Hz}}>1\)) rises from
\SI{7}{\percent} of zero-offset captures (\(n=46\); the band is noisy but
not amplifying) through \SI{72}{\percent} at \(|V_\mathrm{off}|=\SI{1.4}{\volt}\)
(\(n=18\)) to \SI{100}{\percent} at \(|V_\mathrm{off}|\geq\SI{2.4}{\volt}\)
(\(n=20\)), with medians \num{0.46}, \num{1.25}, \num{2.78}, \num{6.69},
and \num{17.3} at \(|V_\mathrm{off}| = 0\), \num{1.4}, \num{2.0}, \num{2.4},
\SI{2.5}{\volt}. The \SI{200}{\hertz} boundary point, captured twice per
condition minutes apart, repeats to a median \SI{1.2}{\percent} (\(n=95\)),
so the gain values are not acquisition noise. Because \(|H|>1\) at the drive
frequency is impossible for a passive divider into a passive load, the biased
junction is an \emph{active} element at these frequencies: the drive slowly
modulates the ionic conductance, and that modulation mixes the DC bias up to
the fundamental, transferring power from the bias into the signal band. The
conclusion is robust to the demodulator amplitude convention (a worst-case
\(\sqrt{2}\) would reduce \(\approx\!18\) to \(\approx\!13\)). Across
\num{32} matched \(\pm\) pairs the gain is close to polarity-symmetric, with
a median \SI{11}{\percent} positive-offset excess (Wilcoxon \(p=0.013\)),
far weaker than the DC potentiation asymmetry of
\siCompRef{app:ch4_gold_dynamics}.

\paragraph{L4, ageing.} The passive plateau is stable within pixel where
days repeat: \ce{K} pixels move from \num{0.444} (day 3) to
\numrange{0.450}{0.451} (day 9), \ce{Na} pixels stay within
\(\pm\SI{2}{\percent}\) over the same week, while the \ce{Li} pixels rise by
\(\approx\!\SI{15}{\percent}\) from day 4 (\numrange{0.35}{0.37}) to day 10
(\(\approx\!\num{0.42}\)) and the day-23 pixel sits at \num{0.39}. One
pixel-day deviates (unkK2-L5 day 4, plateau \num{0.55} at \(-17\) to
\(-21^{\circ}\) phase): that pixel had served as the capacitor-series
workhorse the previous day, so its elevated, resistive plateau is read as a
conditioned (potentiated) state, not an artefact, it is excluded from the
plateau statistics above. Bias-activated gain is present at every age at
which offsets were applied (days 4, 9/10, and 23), but the offset--amplitude
protocol changed between sessions, so no matched-condition ageing comparison
of the gain itself is possible.

\paragraph{L5, DC monitor.} The DC component at the output node follows
the offset linearly in magnitude and sign across the day-23 series
(\cref{tab:ch4_lockin_gain}, last column; \(\approx\!1.4\times\) the offset
in the export's convention), confirming that under bias the junction
DC-conducts well below the \SI{10}{\mega\ohm} input impedance, a
\(\sim\!\si{\mega\ohm}\)-scale biased state, the same order as the
constant-bias end states of the DC corpus
(\siCompRef{app:ch4_gold_dynamics}). At zero offset the rectified DC is not
significantly different from zero (median \SI{-0.18}{\volt}, \SI{61}{\percent}
of \(n=46\) captures negative, binomial \(p=0.18\)).

\paragraph{Bounds.} One batch, one substrate per salt (cation and substrate
fully confounded), and one pixel for the day-23 series. The sweeps descend in
frequency, so every low-frequency point follows minutes of conditioning under
the same offset, the gain states are conditioned steady states, not
pristine ones, and the low-frequency points at \emph{zero} offset are visibly
non-stationary (state drift during the point). Absolute \(|H|\) and DC
values inherit the demodulator amplitude convention (at most a factor
\(\sqrt{2}\); all gain and symmetry statements are ratios at fixed channel
and unaffected); \(C_\mathrm{dev}\) is correspondingly approximate. The
substrate identities are inferred, as stated under provenance.

\section{Absolute Conductance Tests Simple Dilution}\label{app:ch4_percolation}

The working mechanism of \cref{sec:ch4_discussion} initially read the window
contraction at high \PEO{} as a \emph{dilution} of the \SY{}
electronic-percolation network. That picture makes a direct, falsifiable
prediction on a quantity the peak-normalised dynamics discard: if added
ion-transport polymer simply interrupted the electronic network, the
\emph{absolute} on-state conductance should \emph{fall} with \PEO{}. It does the
opposite. Within the replicated \(r_{\PEO{}}=\numlist{0.3;0.6;1.2}\) grid, the
on-state conductance (HYST conductance at the sweep maximum, substrate medians,
\(n=43\)) has a positive overall association with \PEO{}: Spearman
\(\rho=+0.61\) (\(p<0.001\)) and a log--log slope of approximately \(+2.5\)
(\cref{fig:ch4_percolation}). The cell-median conductance is not strictly
monotone between \PEO{}~\num{0.6} and \num{1.2}. The result therefore rejects the
direction predicted by simple dilution, but it does not identify a unique
conductance law. Thickness also rises with \PEO{} (\cref{app:ch4_confounds}), so
geometry remains a measured covariate rather than an explanation assumed away.

The literal dilution reading is therefore inconsistent with the observed sign.
A parallel, weakly switchable path in the ion-rich phase that raises the
conductance floor is one candidate interpretation carried into
\cref{sec:ch4_discussion}. Since the on--off ratio falls with \PEO{}, this
candidate requires off-state conductance to rise faster than on-state
conductance. The present data do not distinguish it from every alternative
mixed-conduction mechanism.

\section{Impedance--Memory Timescale Check}\label{app:ch4_timescale}

Section~\ref{sec:ch4_eis} notes that the EIS resistance \emph{scale} tracks the
fading-memory time across composition, while the small-signal apex \emph{frequency}
does not: for the \(n=23\) substrates with matched EIS and decay data, the median
Nyquist-apex time is
\(\tau_{RC}=1/(2\pi f_{\mathrm{apex}})\approx\SI{0.089}{\second}\), more than an
order of magnitude shorter than the seconds-long \(t_{1/2}\). The following is
an order-of-magnitude consistency check, not an identification of a common
physical clock.

The apex \(RC\) is set by the geometric capacitance
\(C_{\mathrm{geo}}=1/(2\pi f_{\mathrm{apex}}Z^{\mathrm{apex}}_{\mathrm{real}})\).
The forgetting of a \emph{written} state, by contrast, is the relaxation of a
displaced ionic profile and is governed by the much larger chemical/double-layer
capacitance \(C_{\mathrm{chem}}\) of the ion-rich film. Measuring
\(C_{\mathrm{chem}}\) independently from the low-frequency branch of the same
spectra (\(C_{\mathrm{lf}}=-1/(2\pi f Z_{\mathrm{imag}})\) at the lowest measured
frequency) gives a median capacitance enhancement
\(C_{\mathrm{lf}}/C_{\mathrm{geo}}\approx69\) (\(n=23\)). Multiplying this ratio
by the median apex time gives \(\approx\SI{6.2}{\second}\), close to the measured
median \(t_{1/2}\approx\SI{6.0}{\second}\). Because the low-frequency branch is
finite-bandwidth, the capacitance estimate is model-dependent, and the EIS and
large-signal decay probe different operating states, this numerical agreement is
supporting consistency only. It neither proves that one capacitance sets the
decay nor upgrades the resistance correlation to a unique mechanism.

\section{Repeatability Checks}\label{app:ch4_reproducibility}

A physical reservoir requires elements that are diverse \emph{across} the bank but
\emph{individually reproducible}. The chapter documents the diversity at length;
this section quantifies the reproducibility. The fading-memory protocol measured a
single junction per device, so a population-level repeat-\emph{decay}
reproducibility cannot be estimated, only one series device carries two
junctions, and there the model-free \(t_{1/2}\) agrees to
\(\sigma(\ln t_{1/2})\approx0.19\); broader repeat-decay statistics are a stated
gap (\cref{sec:ch4_limitations}). The steady-state switching window, however, is
recorded over several sweeps per junction (median \(\approx5\)), giving a
genuine junction-level sweep reproducibility: the sweep-to-sweep scatter
of the on--off ratio is \(\sigma(\ln R_{\mathrm{on/off}})\approx0.14\) across
\num{142} series junctions, i.e.\ each junction reproduces its switching window to
about \SI{14}{\percent} within that protocol. This statistic does not establish
repeat-decay reproducibility. It is smaller than the across-composition
timescale span (\(\Delta\ln t_{1/2}\approx1.95\),
\cref{app:ch4_resource}), but the comparison crosses two different observables
and is therefore descriptive.

\section{Heterogeneous Timescales}\label{app:ch4_resource}

The chapter treats the spread of fading-memory times as a possible resource. The
cell-median \(t_{1/2}\) spans \(\Delta\ln t_{1/2}\approx1.95\) across the
composition series, while the robust between-substrate scatter within a cell is
\(\sigma_{\mathrm{within}}(\ln t_{1/2})\approx0.264\). If, as a design heuristic,
two bands are considered distinguishable when separated by between one and two
within-cell standard deviations, then
\(N_{\mathrm{eff}}=1+\Delta\ln t_{1/2}/(k\sigma_{\mathrm{within}})\) gives about
\numrange{2.15}{4.69} for \(k=2\) to \(1\). This is not an information-capacity
estimate: it ignores task, noise correlations, readout, and finite sampling. It
only indicates that the measured composition span can plausibly supply a small
number of separated fading-memory bands for the modelling in \cref{ch:temporal}.

\paragraph{Comparison of within-curve and between-substrate dispersion.} The
median identified stretch gives \(1-\beta\approx0.387\), while the robust
between-substrate statistic is \(\sigma(\ln t_{1/2})\approx0.264\). These numbers
are of similar order, but they are not commensurate: \(1-\beta\) is a
dimensionless shape parameter of one fitted decay, whereas
\(\sigma(\ln t_{1/2})\) is a population dispersion. Their similarity neither
demonstrates a common activation-barrier distribution nor excludes independent
sources of variation.
